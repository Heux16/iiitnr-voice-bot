# Academic Ordinances, Regulations & Student Guidelines

Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee International Institute of Information Technology, Naya Raipur (DSPM-IIITNR).
Official Academic Rules, Ordinances, Grading Standards, and Student Policies governing Undergraduate (B.Tech), Postgraduate (M.Tech), and Ph.D. Programs.

<!-- page: 1 -->

## Section 1: Academic Programs & Degree Requirements

1. **Undergraduate Programs**: The Institute offers four-year Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) degree programs in:
   - Computer Science & Engineering (CSE)
   - Data Science & Artificial Intelligence (DSAI)
   - Electronics & Communication Engineering (ECE)
2. **Graduation Requirements**: To qualify for the award of the B.Tech degree, a student must:
   - Successfully complete all mandatory core courses, professional electives, open electives, project works, and humanities courses specified in the curriculum.
   - Earn a minimum total of **160 academic credits**.
   - Maintain a minimum final Cumulative Performance Index (**CPI**) of **5.0** on a 10.0-point grading scale.
   - Have no pending disciplinary actions or outstanding financial dues.
3. **Maximum Duration of Degree**: The standard duration of the B.Tech program is four academic years (8 semesters). The maximum permitted time span to fulfill all graduation requirements and earn the B.Tech degree is **6 academic years (12 semesters)** from the date of initial admission. Any student failing to complete requirements within 6 years shall be disqualified from the program.

## Section 2: Attendance Regulations & Medical Condonation

1. **Mandatory Attendance Rule**: All registered students are expected to attend every lecture, tutorial, and laboratory session. A minimum mandatory attendance of **75%** is strictly required in each individual course to be eligible to sit for the End-Semester Examination.
2. **Attendance Condonation on Medical & Official Grounds**:
   - A relaxation (condonation) of up to a maximum of **10%** in attendance (allowing a minimum attendance floor of **65%**) may be granted by the Competent Authority (Dean of Academic Affairs) on verified medical grounds (serious illness or hospitalization) or for representing the Institute in official sports, academic conferences, or cultural competitions.
   - In cases of medical illness, the student or their guardian must submit a formal medical leave application supported by valid medical certificates and hospital discharge summaries to the Academic Affairs Office within **7 days** of returning to campus.
3. **Debarment / Grade Penalty**: Any student whose attendance in a course falls below 75% (or below 65% after approved medical condonation) will be summarily debarred from appearing in the End-Semester Examination of that course and shall be awarded an '**F**' (Fail) or '**XX**' grade due to attendance shortage. Such a course must be repeated in a subsequent semester.

## Section 3: 10-Point Letter Grading Scale & Evaluation

The Institute follows a credit-based continuous evaluation and 10-point letter grading system. Academic performance in each course is represented by letter grades with corresponding grade points:

| Letter Grade | Qualitative Description | Grade Points |
|---|---|---|
| **A+** | Outstanding | 10 |
| **A** | Excellent | 9 |
| **B+** | Very Good | 8 |
| **B** | Good | 7 |
| **C+** | Average | 6 |
| **C** | Below Average | 5 |
| **D** | Marginal / Minimum Pass | 4 |
| **F** | Fail (Unsatisfactory) | 0 |
| **I** | Incomplete (Temporary) | 0 |
| **AU** | Audit Course Cleared | Satisfactory / No Points |

1. **Minimum Passing Grade**: Grade **'D'** (4 grade points) is the minimum letter grade required to pass and clear any academic course. A student receiving an '**F**' grade earns 0 credits and must clear the course by re-registering or appearing in a supplementary examination.
2. **Temporary 'I' Grade**: The '**I**' (Incomplete) grade is awarded temporarily to a student who has missed the end-semester examination due to documented, approved medical emergencies. The 'I' grade is converted into a regular letter grade upon completion of the make-up examination.

## Section 4: Performance Indices (SPI & CPI Calculation)

Academic evaluation across semesters is measured using the Semester Performance Index (SPI) and Cumulative Performance Index (CPI):

1. **Semester Performance Index (SPI)**: SPI measures the student's academic performance in a specific semester. It is computed as the credit-weighted average of grade points earned in all courses registered during that semester:
   $$\text{SPI} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i}$$
   where:
   - $n$ is the total number of credit courses registered in that semester,
   - $C_i$ is the number of credits assigned to course $i$,
   - $G_i$ is the grade point earned in course $i$.
2. **Cumulative Performance Index (CPI)**: CPI reflects the overall academic performance from the first semester up to the current semester. It is computed as the cumulative weighted average of grade points over all credit courses taken to date:
   $$\text{CPI} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m} (C_j \times G_j)}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} C_j}$$
   where $m$ is the total number of credit courses completed up to the evaluation point.
3. Both SPI and CPI are rounded to two decimal places and appear on official academic transcripts.

## Section 5: Branch Sliding & Branch Change Regulations

Undergraduate students have a one-time opportunity to change their branch of study (branch sliding) between Computer Science & Engineering (CSE), Data Science & Artificial Intelligence (DSAI), and Electronics & Communication Engineering (ECE) subject to the following rules:

1. **Timing**: Branch sliding is permitted ONLY once, at the conclusion of the **First Academic Year (end of Semester II)**.
2. **Eligibility Criteria**:
   - The applicant must have cleared all registered courses in Semester I and Semester II in the first attempt with **no active backlogs or 'F' grades**.
   - The student must have attained a minimum **CPI of 8.0** at the end of the second semester.
3. **Merit-Based Allocation & Capacity Constraints**:
   - Branch sliding applications are ranked strictly in order of merit based on the Cumulative Performance Index (CPI) at the end of Semester II.
   - The revised strength of any branch after branch change must not exceed **110% of the sanctioned intake** for that branch.
   - The revised strength of any branch after branch change must not fall below **80% of the sanctioned intake** for that branch.
   - Once a branch change is approved and notified, it is final and irrevocable.

## Section 6: Academic Probation, Make-Up Exams & Course Re-evaluation

1. **Academic Probation Policy**:
   - An undergraduate student whose Semester Performance Index (**SPI**) or Cumulative Performance Index (**CPI**) falls below **5.0** at the end of any semester is automatically placed on **Academic Probation**.
   - A student on academic probation is placed under academic counseling, assigned a faculty mentor, and permitted a restricted/reduced credit load in the subsequent semester.
   - If a student continues to remain on academic probation for two consecutive semesters without adequate improvement, their case is referred to the Senate Academic Review Committee for probation extension or termination of registration.
2. **Make-Up Examination Policy**:
   - A student who is unable to appear for the scheduled Mid-Semester or End-Semester examination due to serious medical illness, hospitalization, or tragic bereavement in the immediate family may be granted permission to take a Make-Up Examination.
   - The student or their parent/guardian must submit a formal written application with authentic medical / verifying proof to the Academic Section within **3 days** of the missed examination date.
   - Make-up examinations are conducted before the commencement of the next academic semester, and the interim 'I' grade is updated based on marks scored.
3. **Answer Script Inspection & Re-evaluation**:
   - Evaluated answer booklets are made available for student inspection during scheduled grade display sessions following the end-semester exams.
   - If a student detects totaling errors, unevaluated answers, or evaluation discrepancies, they can submit a formal application for re-evaluation / rechecking through the Academic Section within **5 working days** of grade declaration along with the prescribed verification fee.

## Section 7: Study Leave, Audit Courses, Minor Specialization & Internships

1. **Formal Study Leave**: A student may be granted formal study leave or permission for a temporary withdrawal from academic studies for valid medical reasons or exceptional personal circumstances. The maximum permitted duration of formal study leave is **two semesters (1 academic year)** during the entire degree program, subject to approval by the Institute Senate.
2. **Audit Courses Policy**:
   - A student may register for up to two audit courses per semester with prior approval of the course instructor and faculty advisor.
   - Audit courses carry no grade points and do not count toward SPI or CPI calculations.
   - To earn an '**AU**' (Audit Cleared) entry on the official transcript, the student must satisfy the mandatory **75% attendance** rule and complete basic coursework assigned by the instructor.
3. **Minor Specialization**:
   - B.Tech students who maintain a minimum CPI of **7.0** at the end of Semester IV can opt for a Minor Specialization in emerging interdisciplinary domains (such as AI & Machine Learning, Cyber Security, or Management).
   - Completing a Minor requires earning an additional **16 to 20 academic credits** in prescribed discipline electives between Semesters V and VIII.
4. **Mandatory Summer Internship**:
   - All undergraduate students must complete a mandatory **6 to 8 weeks** summer industrial or research internship following their 6th semester.
   - The internship carries academic credits and is evaluated through external employer ratings, a detailed project report, and an internal departmental viva voce examination.

## Section 8: Campus Living, Hostel Rules & Healthcare Facilities

1. **Hostel Residency & Curfew Regulations**:
   - IIIT Naya Raipur is a fully residential campus. Hostel residency is compulsory for all admitted undergraduate and postgraduate students.
   - **Hostel Curfew & Night Entry**: All resident students must return to the hostel campus by **10:00 PM** daily.
   - **Night Gate Pass**: Any student needing to leave campus overnight or return after curfew hours must obtain a verified **Night Gate Pass** approved by the Hostel Warden / Chief Warden in advance.
2. **Campus Health Center, Dispensary & 24x7 Ambulance**:
   - The Institute operates a dedicated on-campus **Health Center (Dispensary)** located in the residential zone.
   - Facilities include a resident Medical Officer (Doctor), trained nursing staff, emergency first-aid, routine diagnostic checks, and essential medications.
   - A dedicated **24x7 emergency ambulance** is stationed on campus round the clock for immediate transport of sick or injured students to premier multispecialty hospitals in Raipur and Atal Nagar (such as AIIMS Raipur and Balaji Hospital).
   - Emergency medical helpline numbers are prominently posted in all hostel lobbies and security control gates.
